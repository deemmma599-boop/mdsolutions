the definition

What Android & iOS app development means

Android and iOS app development is the process of designing, building, testing and publishing a mobile application for the two dominant smartphone platforms. Android runs on phones from Samsung, Google, Huawei and many others; iOS runs on Apple's iPhone. Together they represent virtually the entire smartphone market — which is why most serious apps launch on both.

A mobile app is fundamentally different from a mobile website. It's installed on the device, can use the camera, GPS, Bluetooth, notifications, biometrics and offline storage, and it stays on the user's home screen — creating an ongoing relationship rather than a one‑off visit. That makes apps powerful for engagement, retention and repeat transactions.

There are two broad ways to build one. Native apps are written separately for each platform using Apple's Swift and Google's Kotlin — the highest performance and deepest device integration. Cross‑platform apps are written once in a shared framework (React Native or Flutter) and compiled for both platforms — faster to build, cheaper to maintain, and near‑native for most business applications.

Our approach is pragmatic: we choose the method that matches your goals, timeline and budget — then design an app that feels fast, fluid and intuitive on whichever platform your users hold in their hands.

At a glance

choose the right path

Native vs cross‑platform: which is right for you?

There is no universal "better" — only the right fit for your app's demands. Here's the honest comparison we walk every client through.

Native development

Two codebases. Maximum performance and platform fidelity.

Best when you need

  • Heavy graphics, games, AR/VR or real‑time processing.
  • Deep device integration: advanced camera, Bluetooth, sensors, wearables.
  • Platform‑perfect UI that matches each OS's native patterns exactly.
  • Long‑term products where performance and scalability are critical.
  • Maximum reliability across OS version upgrades.

Trade-offs

  • Two separate codebases — higher build and maintenance cost.
  • Longer initial timeline and larger team.
Languages & tools: Swift + SwiftUI (iOS), Kotlin + Jetpack Compose (Android), Xcode, Android Studio.

Cross‑platform development

One codebase. Both stores. Near‑native for most business apps.

Best when you need

  • Both platforms launched quickly from a single codebase.
  • Business apps, marketplaces, booking, dashboards, content and social features.
  • Lower development and maintenance cost for equivalent scope.
  • A consistent brand experience across Android and iOS.
  • Faster iteration — one change ships to both platforms.

Trade-offs

  • Extremely graphics‑heavy or sensor‑intensive apps may still need native modules.
  • Minor platform quirks require occasional per‑platform tuning.
Frameworks: React Native (JavaScript/TypeScript) and Flutter (Dart) — both production‑proven for large apps.

faq

Android & iOS app questions, answered

Should I build a native app or a cross‑platform app?

Choose native (Swift for iOS, Kotlin for Android) when you need maximum performance, deep device integration, advanced camera/AR/Bluetooth features, or platform‑perfect UI. Choose cross‑platform (React Native or Flutter) when you want one codebase for both stores, faster time‑to‑market and lower cost, with near‑native performance for most business apps.

How long does it take to build a mobile app?

A focused MVP typically takes 8 to 16 weeks. Cross‑platform MVPs sit at the lower end; fully native apps with complex integrations, multiple user roles or advanced device features take longer. A simple utility app can be faster; enterprise apps with backend systems can take 4 to 6 months.

How much does Android and iOS app development cost?

Cost depends on complexity, number of platforms, backend requirements and integrations. A cross‑platform MVP is significantly less than two separate native apps. We scope after a discovery workshop and provide a fixed‑scope quote before development begins.

Will my app be published on both the App Store and Google Play?

Yes. We handle the full submission process on both stores: preparing metadata, screenshots, privacy declarations and app review compliance. You own the developer accounts; we manage the technical submission and guide you through approvals.

Can you maintain my app after launch?

Yes. We offer post‑launch support retainers covering OS updates, bug fixes, dependency upgrades and feature iterations. Mobile apps require ongoing maintenance as iOS and Android platforms release new versions.

Do you build apps with user accounts and payments?

Yes. We build authentication (email, social, biometric), subscription and one‑time payments (Apple In‑App Purchase, Google Play Billing, Stripe), push notifications and secure API integration.

Will my app work offline?

Where it makes sense, yes. We build offline‑first experiences with local caching and background sync so users can continue working without a connection, with data syncing when connectivity returns.

Do you design the app UI as well as build it?

Yes. We handle the full journey: discovery and strategy, UX flows, UI design, prototyping, development, testing and store launch. You can also bring existing designs and we build to them.
